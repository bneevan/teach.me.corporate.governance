Lesson 02 · About 8 minutes · India as the anchor

Authority comes before votes

Your tangible win: decide whether a routine purchase belongs with management or needs escalation, without assuming that the largest shareholder decides everything.

First, retrieve—not reread

From memory: if a director's relative offers the best deal, what remains necessary before the company proceeds? Say your answer before opening the reminder.

Review the previous principle

The relationship remains a conflict even if the deal is attractive. It must be disclosed and managed under applicable rules. A company may accept a transaction involving a conflict without accepting conflicted decision-making. Proper procedure cannot legalize a prohibited transaction.

1. Three questions, in this order

QuestionMeaning
Who may decide?Identify the authorized body or person and any required additional approvals.
How must they decide?Apply notice, information, quorum, participation, voting, and record requirements as relevant.
Is the decision substantively lawful?Authority and procedure do not excuse unlawful conduct or breaches of duty.

A vote is a mechanism within an authority structure; it does not create unlimited authority. Under India's Companies Act, section 179(1) gives the board broad powers, subject to law and company documents, and excludes matters reserved to the company in general meeting.

2. A company does not vote on every purchase

Boards could not function if they approved every laptop, invoice, and travel booking. Management can act within valid delegated authority. Some matters remain reserved for the board; others require shareholder approval. Committee approvals may also be needed.

Here is an invented internal policy for our exercise. These rupee amounts are not statutory thresholds:

DecisionCompany's assumed internal route
Routine, budgeted purchase up to ₹5 lakhCEO may approve
Routine purchase above ₹5 lakhBoard approval required
Purchase involving a director's relativeEscalate for conflict review and determination of required approvals, regardless of amount

Assume this policy is validly adopted and all other requirements are met. A budgeted ₹3 lakh purchase from an unrelated supplier can stay with the CEO. A ₹7 lakh purchase exceeds this delegation and must go to the board. A ₹3 lakh purchase from the founder's brother triggers the conflict route despite being below the spending limit.

A spending limit is not a blanket permission. Amount, type of decision, budget, conflicts, and applicable rules can each constrain authority.

3. Ownership votes and board votes are different

Recall Meera: she owns 60% of voting shares and holds one seat on a five-person board.

  • At a shareholder decision, voting rights depend on the shares and applicable rules.
  • At a board decision, her shareholding does not turn her board vote into 60% of the directors' votes.
  • For the following example only, assume five eligible directors, all present, one vote each, a simple-majority rule, no conflicts, and no other restrictions. Three votes in favour and two against can approve a matter within the board's authority. Unanimity is not necessary.

Consensus is a way to seek agreement; it is not a universal legal requirement. In real cases, check the applicable decision rules. In India, sections 173–175 address meetings, quorum, and resolutions by circulation; section 175 expressly refers to a majority of directors entitled to vote for circulation resolutions. Section 179(3) requires specified powers to be exercised through resolutions at board meetings, subject to its delegation provisions. Do not assume every matter can be approved through the same channel.

Quorum means the minimum qualifying participation needed to conduct business, not the number of votes needed to win. We will cover calculations and exceptions separately.

4. India versus Germany: why titles can mislead

India: ordinary single-board mapGerman dual-board framework
The board can include executive and non-executive directors; management acts within its authority.A Management Board and a separate Supervisory Board have distinct management and supervisory roles.

The German Corporate Governance Code foreword describes this dual structure. It concerns the listed-company framework examined, not every German business form. The practical lesson: translate functions, not just titles. When comparing a decision with India, ask which body manages, which supervises, and what approvals apply—rather than treating every body called a board as interchangeable.

5. Apply the policy

Use the invented policy above, not legal thresholds from memory. Give your reasoning in chat before checking the feedback.

1. A routine budgeted purchase costs ₹4 lakh, with no conflicts or other restrictions. Who may approve?

2. The same ₹4 lakh purchase is from Meera's brother's business. What changes?

3. A matter within board authority receives three votes for and two against, with Meera opposing. All five eligible directors are present; the assumed rule is one vote each and simple majority. What follows?

One short primary-source reading

Read section 179(1) of the Companies Act (printed pages 120–121 in the reviewed compilation), including its provisos. Look for the difference between broad board powers and matters reserved to the company in general meeting. You do not need to memorize the lists.

Ask your teacher about anything unclear. Send the three choices with one sentence each explaining why. If reading legislation feels difficult, ask for a plain-language walkthrough.
