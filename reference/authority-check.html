Reference 02

Authority before votes

First identify who may decide. Then determine how that person or body must decide. Check substantive lawfulness as well.

  1. Classify the action. What is being authorized: a routine purchase, borrowing, a major transaction, or something else?
  2. Check sources and limits of authority. Applicable law and rules, company constitutional documents, reserved matters, board decisions, and valid delegations. An internal policy cannot override the law.
  3. Check special triggers. Conflicts, related parties, amount, budget, and any required committee or shareholder approval. More than one approval may be necessary.
  4. Follow the correct process. Information, notice, quorum, eligible participation, voting, records, and disclosures as applicable. Quorum and voting threshold are different concepts.
  5. Do not confuse the hats. Ownership rights, a director's vote, and executive authority are different. A 60% shareholding does not automatically mean 60% of board votes.

This is a reasoning aid, not a complete compliance checklist or legal opinion. Decision-process integrity and commercial merits are separate questions.

India source

Companies Act 2013: sections 173–175 (meetings, quorum, circulation), 179 (powers and limits), and 180 (specified restrictions). Applicable rules and exceptions must also be checked.
