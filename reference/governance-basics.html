Reference 01 · Working glossary

Authority, oversight, accountability

Corporate governance
The system through which a company is directed, controlled, and held accountable: allocation of authority, oversight, decision processes, and safeguards.
Management
Running the business and implementing strategy within delegated authority.
Shareholder / member
A holder of shares or membership rights. Rights depend on law, share class, and company documents. Owning shares is not directly owning each company asset.
Board of directors
The collective governing body with powers and duties established by law and company documents. Individual directors do not automatically possess the board's collective authority.
Chair
Leads the board's work; not necessarily the CEO or a unilateral superior to the board.
CEO / managing director
An executive leadership role. Exact legal status and powers vary; a managing director may also be a director. One person can wear multiple hats.
Independent director
A director meeting applicable independence criteria, intended to support objective oversight. Not a guarantee of sound judgment.
Promoter
A defined Indian legal/regulatory category that may involve formation, identification, control, or influence. Not simply another word for CEO or majority shareholder. Exact classification requires the applicable definition.
Stakeholder
A person or group affected by, or with an interest in, the company. Not everyone has the same legal rights or board representation.
Internal control
A process or safeguard supporting reliable operations, reporting, or compliance—for example, payment approval limits and reconciliation.
Related-party transaction
A transaction involving a relationship specified by applicable law or rules. Relationships can create conflicts; exact definitions and approval requirements vary.
Accountability versus liability
Accountability means having to explain and answer for conduct; legal liability depends on applicable law and facts. A bad outcome does not automatically make every director personally liable.

Basis: India Companies Act, particularly sections 2, 166, 177–180; Delaware sections 141–142; UK Code. These are introductory working definitions, not substitutes for statutory definitions.
